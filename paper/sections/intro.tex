\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A coin flip is not a red light.

A risk score computed at a rural antenatal visit is read by at least three
people. A clinician wants the decomposition and can argue with it. A
community health worker wants to know whether to refer this mother and what
to write down. The mother wants to know what to do next, and she may not read
at all, in which case the score reaches her as a colour and a spoken sentence
or it does not reach her.

Most work on explaining a model to several audiences varies how much of the
explanation each one gets. That leaves a second question open. When the model
is not confident, which assertion should each channel carry?

Consider a case from the dataset we use. The model puts it at $0.013$,
$0.487$ and $0.499$ across low, mid and high risk. The top two are separated
by $0.012$, and the model is wrong: the record is true mid-risk. A clinician
shown that split can see the call was nearly a tie. The mother, at the end of
the same pipeline, has no way to discount anything, and if the rendering
follows the argmax alone she is the one who receives an emergency generated
by a coin flip.

Our response, which we call Three Voices, makes the abstention decision part
of the rendering rather than part of the model. One band is computed per
prediction from the class probabilities, and the highest-severity signal is
raised only when the model is not on a knife edge. Below the threshold the
mother's channel and the health worker's are both derated, while the
clinician's is untouched. \textbf{The derate is red to amber, never
red to green.} Amber still says \emph{needs follow-up} and still sends her to
a health worker. Nothing in this design removes a mother from care, and a
reader who takes ``not an emergency'' to mean ``not a concern'' has read it
backwards.

\subsection{What we claim, and what we do not}

We contribute three things, and they are different kinds of object. In the
terms HCI uses for its own contribution
types~\citep{wobbrock2016contributions}, two are artifact contributions --- a
rendering mechanism and the pipeline that implements it --- and the third is
an empirical one, a measurement on the benchmark underneath both.

\paragraph{A mechanism: abstention graded across stakeholder channels.}
The decision to withhold is taken once, from the model's output, then rendered
as a different assertion for each reader rather than as the same assertion
softened. The gate is a rule over class probabilities, stated with its grading
in Section~\ref{sec:tiers} and reproducible from the committed tables.

\paragraph{An artifact: an auditable rendering pipeline with no language
model at inference.} Every string a health worker or a mother receives is a
fixed template filled from the case's attributions. Nothing is generated at
inference, so the rendering can be regenerated, diffed and checked line by
line by someone who does not trust us, and there is nothing in it to
hallucinate. We pay for that in fluency and coverage, as
Section~\ref{sec:rw-stakeholder} sets out.

\paragraph{A measurement: leakage-aware re-benchmarking of a saturated public
dataset.} Of the 1012 UCI Maternal Health Risk rows that survive a two-row
plausibility filter, 561 are exact duplicates of an earlier row, which can
place the same record in a training fold and the test fold of the same
split. Scoring the identical model under the identical
cross-validation with those rows retained moves accuracy from $0.641$ to
$0.854$ and macro-recall from $0.580$ to $0.859$. The inflated accuracy lands
inside the band the literature reports for this dataset, which is the
comparison Section~\ref{sec:data-leakage} sets out in full. Kapoor and
Narayanan set out leakage's taxonomy and its place in the reproducibility
crisis, and Rosenblatt et al.\ show the inflation is worst on small datasets,
which is our case~\citep{kapoor2023leakage,rosenblatt2024leakage}. We give it
a result's billing because a reader comparing our headline number against the
published band needs it to make sense of either.

Three boundaries go with those claims. \textbf{We do not claim that
explanations should be audience-specific}, which was settled before
us~\citep{arya2019explanation,imrie2023multiple,kim2024stakeholder}.
\textbf{We do not claim the gating mechanism.} Declining to answer under
uncertainty has a long lineage in selective prediction and learning to defer,
and our fixed-margin rule is a hand-set and cruder instance of
it~\citep{hendrickx2024reject,mozannar2020consistent}. \textbf{We do not
claim a modelling contribution.} Fitting a classifier with SHAP attributions
to this dataset is saturated~\citep{mamun2025identification,
maisoon2026stratification}, and Section~\ref{sec:rw-dataset} sets out why we
build on that pipeline as a vehicle. On the audit we claim the measurement:
the duplicates are the kind of thing noticed in a notebook and never written
up, and we report what they cost rather than asserting we found them first.

\subsection{What kind of evidence this is}

No mother, no community health worker and no clinician has used any of these
renderings, and we would rather say so here than let a reader discover it in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

Our evaluation is a formative, single-evaluator heuristic critique of our own
artifact. It found five defects --- including a driver list that could print
a reading arguing against its own prediction under a heading announcing it as
a flag --- and all five were fixed before this paper was written. It is an
instrument that improved the design, not evidence that any tier helps anyone,
and Section~\ref{sec:eval} reports those defects first.

We ask to be judged on the following. The rendering rule and the pipeline that
implements it are the object; the artifact is validated by formative
inspection against published usability and accessibility criteria; the leakage
effect is quantified and reproducible in either direction from a committed
table. The data is public, the task is generic maternal-health risk, and
nothing here predicts an outcome for a real patient. Whether any of it helps a
mother, a health worker or a clinician is a question Section~\ref{sec:future}
takes up rather than one this paper answers.
