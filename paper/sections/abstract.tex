\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Purpose.} A maternal-health risk model splits a case
$0.487$ against $0.499$ between adjacent risk levels. A clinician can
read that margin and discount it; a mother handed a red alarm for the same
prediction cannot. We ask what each reader of an uncertain prediction is
entitled to be told.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.} One prediction is rendered for three readers,
gated on the model's uncertainty: below a fixed top-two margin the
highest-severity signal is withheld from the mother's channel and from no
other. The derate is red to amber, never red to green, and amber still says
\emph{needs follow-up}. Every string is a deterministic template over SHAP
attributions, with no language model at inference. This is a design and
feasibility artifact; its evaluation is a formative single-evaluator
inspection of our own renderings rather than a user study.

\noindent\textbf{Results.} The inspection found five defects in our own
artifact, all corrected. It is not evidence that the tiers help
anyone; nobody in the three groups has used the system. Retaining this
benchmark's 561 duplicate rows moves macro-recall from
$0.580$ to $0.859$, a measurement we report, not a discovery we claim.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.} The work delivers an auditable,
uncertainty-gated rendering discipline with no language model at inference,
and a leakage-aware re-benchmarking of a saturated public dataset. Abstention
graded by stakeholder vulnerability is an intersection of two established
literatures, not the gating mechanism. The data is public, the task is
generic maternal-health risk, we make no clinical-validity claim, and user
studies are the next step, gated on ethics.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} explainable AI; uncertainty; selective
prediction; maternal health; community health workers; risk communication.
\end{abstract}
