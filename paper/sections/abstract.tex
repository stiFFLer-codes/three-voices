\begin{abstract}
\noindent Three Voices renders one maternal-health risk prediction as three
action signals: a decomposition for a clinician, a referral card for a
community health worker, and a three-lamp visual with one spoken Hindi
sentence for the mother. One band (green, amber or red) is computed from the
model's class probabilities. When a high-risk call falls within a fixed
top-two margin of 0.15, red is withheld from the health worker and the mother
and replaced by amber, which still asks for follow-up; the clinician sees the
probabilities unchanged. All text comes from fixed templates; SHAP
attributions supply the card's drivers, and no language model runs at
inference. On out-of-fold
scores from the UCI Maternal Health Risk dataset, the gate derates 11 of 114
high-risk calls; 3 of those 11 are truly high-risk, against 78 of the 103 it
keeps. It does not address the 14 high-risk records the model labels low. We
also quantify benchmark leakage: 561 of the 1012 usable rows duplicate an
earlier row, and keeping them raises macro-recall from 0.580 to 0.859 under
the same cross-validation. The evaluation is a formative heuristic inspection
by one author; no user study has been run. Five commands regenerate every
table and rendering.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} explainable AI; uncertainty; selective
prediction; maternal health; community health workers; risk communication.
\end{abstract}
