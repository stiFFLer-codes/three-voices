\begin{abstract}
\noindent Three Voices renders a single maternal-health risk prediction as
three stakeholder-specific action-signals, one each for a clinician, a
community health worker, and a mother, and gates each signal on the model's
own uncertainty. Below a fixed top-two margin the highest-severity signal is
withheld from the mother's channel and from no other: the derate is red to
amber, never red to green, and amber still says \emph{needs follow-up}. Every
string is a deterministic template filled from the case's SHAP attributions,
with no language model at inference, so a rendering can be regenerated and
audited line by line. Code, the rendered artifacts, the cross-validation and
SHAP tables, and the evaluation matrix are archived, and all of it
regenerates from five commands.

This is a design and feasibility artifact. Its evaluation is a
formative single-evaluator heuristic critique of our own renderings, which
found five defects, all of them corrected. That is an instrument that
improved the design, not evidence that any tier helps a reader. No mother,
health worker or clinician has used the system; the data is public, the task
is generic maternal-health risk, and we make no clinical-validity claim. User
studies with the three groups are the next step, gated on ethics clearance.
The abstention idea sits between stakeholder-tailored explanation and
selective prediction, and we claim only their pairing, not either mechanism.

We also report a leakage audit of the UCI Maternal Health Risk
benchmark. It contains 561 exact duplicate rows, and retaining them moves
macro-recall from $0.580$ to $0.859$ under the same cross-validation. That is
a cost we quantify, not a fault we claim to have found first.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} explainable AI; uncertainty; selective
prediction; maternal health; community health workers; risk communication.
\end{abstract}
