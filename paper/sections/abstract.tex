\begin{abstract}
\noindent Three Voices renders a single maternal-health risk prediction as
three stakeholder-specific action-signals, one each for a clinician, a
community health worker, and a mother, and gates each signal on the model's
own uncertainty. Below a fixed top-two margin the highest-severity signal is
withheld from the mother's channel and the health worker's, while the
clinician's view of the split is untouched: the derate is red to amber, never
red to green, and amber still says \emph{needs follow-up}. Every
string is a deterministic template filled from the case's SHAP attributions,
with no language model at inference, so a rendering can be regenerated and
audited line by line. Code, the rendered artifacts, the tables and the
evaluation matrix are archived, and all of it regenerates from five commands.

We also report a leakage audit of the UCI Maternal Health Risk
benchmark. Of the 1012 rows surviving a two-row plausibility filter, 561
are exact duplicates of an earlier row, and retaining them moves macro-recall
from $0.580$ to $0.859$ under the same cross-validation. We quantify that
cost; we do not claim to have found it first.

The abstention rule sits between stakeholder-tailored explanation and
selective prediction; we claim their pairing, not either mechanism. Our
evaluation is a formative single-evaluator heuristic critique of our own
renderings; it found five defects, all corrected. It improved the design; it
is not evidence that any tier helps a reader. No mother, health worker or
clinician has used the system, the data is public, the task
is generic maternal-health risk, and we make no clinical-validity claim; user
studies with the three groups are the next step, gated on ethics clearance.
The paper delivers the rendering mechanism, an auditable implementation,
and a reproducible benchmark measurement.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} explainable AI; uncertainty; selective
prediction; maternal health; community health workers; risk communication.
\end{abstract}
