\section{Three Tiers from One Prediction}
\label{sec:tiers}

\subsection{What the alarm is, and what derating it means}

By \emph{alarm} we mean the highest-severity signal a tier is permitted to
raise: the red lamp and the urgent spoken message for the mother, the
\textsc{high} header on the health worker's card. The rule this section
describes withholds that signal when the model is close to a coin flip, and
substitutes a middle signal that still says \emph{needs follow-up}.

The direction of that substitution matters most, so we state it first: the
derate is red to amber, never red to green. When the model is close to a tie,
its output gives no basis for the loudest signal, and the system falls back to
the one below it, which still sends the mother to a health worker. Nothing
here removes a mother from care.

\subsection{The band rule}
\label{sec:tiers-band}

Each rendering is driven by one band, computed once per prediction from the
class probabilities and shared across all three tiers:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{green} if the predicted class is low risk.
  \item \textsc{red} if the predicted class is high risk \emph{and} the
    top-two probability margin is at least $0.15$.
  \item \textsc{amber} otherwise, which covers every mid-risk prediction
    and every high-risk prediction that sits inside the margin.
\end{itemize}

All probabilities and SHAP values in this section are out of fold: each record
is scored by the cross-validation fold model that did not see it
(Section~\ref{sec:data-model}).

The threshold of $0.15$ is a design constant. We did not tune it, and no
experiment in this paper supports that value over $0.10$ or $0.20$; we return
to what would be needed to justify it in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

The gate is one-sided. The margin test guards the high boundary only: \textsc{green}
follows the argmax with no margin test at all, so a prediction that is
low risk by a hair renders an unqualified all-clear to the mother. That is the
opposite of the asymmetry Section~\ref{sec:data-model} argues for, where
missing a higher-risk mother is the error that matters. Out of fold, 22
low-risk predictions fall inside the margin, and 3 of them are true high-risk
records (Table~\ref{tab:gate}). This version does not
implement the symmetric extension, derating an ungated near-tie
\textsc{green} down to \textsc{amber} as the red-to-amber move already does;
Section~\ref{sec:future} takes it up.

Computing the band once also matters. In the first version of the renderer
the health worker's card banded on its own rule and the mother's lamp banded
on this one, so a single prediction could receive two different bands. Section~\ref{sec:eval} describes how that was caught.

\subsection{One prediction, three renderings}
\label{sec:tiers-render}

Figure~\ref{fig:tiers} shows the same prediction rendered three ways. The
case is row 426 of the deduplicated set, a true mid-risk record the model
calls high risk. Out of fold, its class probabilities are
$0.233 / 0.382 / 0.384$ across low, mid and high, and the top-two margin is
$0.002$. Blood sugar ($+0.227$) and age ($+0.052$) push toward high risk.
Systolic pressure ($-0.072$), heart rate ($-0.050$) and body temperature
($-0.026$) push against it. Diastolic pressure is negligible ($+0.007$).

\paragraph{Clinician.} The full decomposition, unmodified. Signed SHAP
contributions per feature with numeric labels, all three class probabilities,
and the six raw readings in clinical units. The $0.382$ against $0.384$ is
shown as it stands. A reader who can interrogate the model is given what she
needs to do so, including the fact that the call was nearly a tie.

\paragraph{Community health worker.} A card carrying the band header, the
factors behind \emph{this} prediction, and one next step. Two rules shape it.
The factors are local rather than global: naming the dataset's top features
on every card would print the same two words for every mother and explain
nothing about any of them. And only features that push \emph{toward} the
predicted class are listed, ranked by signed contribution, because a card
headed ``what the model flagged'' that lists a reading arguing against its own
prediction misleads the person making the referral. On this case a ranking by
absolute value would have listed systolic pressure, which argues against the
call. The card reads \textsc{elevated --- needs follow-up}, lists raised blood
sugar and older age, and asks the worker to arrange a check-up and share
the readings. Every string is a fixed template. The band selects the card
header, the next step and the mother's spoken sentence, and the SHAP
attributions fill the card's driver list. No language model runs at inference, which is our stated
alternative to LLM-mediated tiering~\citep{bello2025threelevel}: the card can
be regenerated and compared line by line, and it contains no generated text.

\paragraph{Mother-to-be.} A three-lamp visual and one spoken Hindi sentence.
No probability, no percentage, no condition named, and no text required to
use it. The tier answers \emph{what do I do next}, not \emph{how likely is
this}. Here the amber lamp lights and the message says, in Hindi, that some
findings need attention, not to panic, and to get checked soon at her ANM or
nearest health centre. The Hindi
strings were written and reviewed by the second author, a final-year MBBS
student and native Hindi speaker; they are not machine-translated.

Stripping magnitude departs from the risk-communication literature of
Section~\ref{sec:rw-riskcomm}, which recommends conveying magnitude through an
icon array or a visible denominator, and we take that departure on a stated
condition. An icon array conveys a magnitude the model can support. At
$0.382$ against $0.384$ there is no such magnitude to convey, and drawing that
split as 38 chances in 100 would give the reader least able to discount it a
precision the model does not have. When the magnitude is trustworthy it should
be shown; when the gate has fired, the model supports only an ordinal band and
one action. That is the uncertainty argument of Section~\ref{sec:rw-uncertainty} arriving
from the health-communication side, and it remains an argument rather than a
result, since whether the band is understood as intended is untested here.

\subsection{Derating a false alarm}
\label{sec:tiers-failure}

Row 426 shows the rule behaving correctly on a near-tie between mid and high.
Row 325 shows what it is for. That record is true low risk, the model calls it
high risk at $0.411 / 0.148 / 0.441$ across low, mid and high, and the top-two
margin is $0.029$. It belongs to a 17-year-old with a recorded body
temperature of 101\,\textdegree F, and body temperature drives the call
($+0.253$). This is the case Section~\ref{sec:data-age} designates as the
vehicle's failure mode. On the argmax alone she would have received a red lamp
and an urgent spoken message. The gate withheld it and both derated channels
show amber instead.

Amber on a true-low record is still a false referral. The gate made the error
smaller, not correct, and it did nothing about the underlying misprediction,
which the clinician tier carries unmodified here as it does on every case.
It changed only what the mother was told.

Table~\ref{tab:gate} gives the counts. The gate derates 11 of the 114
high-risk calls. Of the 103 calls it keeps as red, 78 are true high-risk
records; of the 11 it derates, only 3 are. Eleven calls are too few to
estimate that difference precisely, and this is one dataset. The 3 derated
high-risk records are the cost of the rule: they receive amber instead of red,
and amber still routes them to a health worker.

The number of red bands changes little with the threshold: 105, 103 and 102
at margins of $0.10$, $0.15$ and $0.20$.

The gate leaves 14 true high-risk records with a green band. Only 3 of them
sit inside the margin, among the 22 low-risk near-ties of
Section~\ref{sec:tiers-band}. The other 11 are outside the $0.15$ margin, with
margins from $0.230$ to $0.880$. Overall, 98 of the 112 true high-risk records receive a band
that routes them to follow-up.
