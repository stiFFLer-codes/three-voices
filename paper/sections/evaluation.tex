\section{Formative Heuristic Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We rated the rendered artifacts against WCAG 2.1 and Nielsen's usability
heuristics: 16 criteria across three tiers, 48 cells, each grounded in a
named detail of a file on disk. Contrast ratios are computed at run time from
the palette constants rather than typed in by hand.

Both instruments are inspection methods, each with a stated procedure and a
literature on what it does and does not catch. Heuristic evaluation was
introduced as a way for evaluators rather than users to find usability
problems in an interface design~\citep{nielsen1990heuristic}, and we rate
against Nielsen's revised heuristics~\citep{nielsen1994enhancing};
conformance inspection against the WCAG 2.1 success criteria is the
corresponding instrument on the accessibility side~\citep{w3c2025wcag21}.
Neither substitutes for users.

This is a single-evaluator, author-conducted, formative assessment. It is not
user testing and it is not validation. Nothing in it is evidence that the
tiers help anyone. What it did was find defects in our own artifact, which is
what we report.

\subsection{What it found}

The first pass ran against the original renderer, on cases selected from
in-sample probabilities. It returned four Fail ratings, three of them on the
health worker's card, arising from five distinct defects. All five were fixed
and the tiers re-rendered, with the cases now scored out of fold
(Section~\ref{sec:data-model}). The ratings below and in
Table~\ref{tab:matrix} describe the corrected artifacts.

\paragraph{Sign-blind driver list.} Features were ranked by absolute
SHAP magnitude with no sign filter, so a reading that argued \emph{against}
the prediction could be printed under a heading announcing it as a flag. On
the original false-positive case the card listed low blood sugar, whose
contribution to the predicted class was $-0.085$. The routine card was worse:
it listed the two features that were the model's reasons for saying \emph{low}
under that same heading, above a next step telling the reader to arrange a
check-up. This is the defect that could have put a wrong cue in front of the
person making a referral, and no model metric could have shown it, because
metrics never read the card.

\paragraph{Two bands for one prediction.} The health worker's card and the
mother's lamp banded independently, so the confident high-risk case rendered
an amber card beside a red lamp: one prediction with two identities. The band
is now computed once and passed to both.

\paragraph{Unmeasured header contrast.} White header text was hardcoded; on
the amber header, which is the state for two of the four rendered cases, it
measured $2.12{:}1$. Ink is now chosen per band by measured contrast, worst
case $5.14{:}1$, and a self-check asserts it.

\paragraph{Direction words without a deadband.} A blood sugar of $7.7$ against
a median of $7.5$ was worded as ``raised''. The word now attaches only outside
$0.25$ IQR of the median.

\paragraph{No provenance on the mother's transcript.} The transcript carried
none, while both other tiers printed the disclaimer. It now records case, band and disclaimer under
an explicit written-not-spoken rule; the spoken line is unchanged, because
appending provenance to every message would bury the one action the tier
exists to deliver.

\subsection{What it could not settle}

Table~\ref{tab:matrix} gives the full matrix. Across the 48 cells the
distribution after those fixes is 29 pass, 12 partial, 1 fail and 6 deferred.
The count is a description of coverage and not a score, and the five defects
above carry more weight. The single failure is the clinician tier shipping as
an image of text. The six deferred cells are all keyboard operability and
assistive-technology semantics, which a static PNG cannot exercise and a
deployed application would.

None of this tells us whether a health worker or a mother understands what
she is handed. Studies with community health workers in India show that how
well they understand an AI explanation depends on its
design~\citep{okolo2024easy,solanokamaiko2024explorable}, so the answer cannot
be assumed. Studies with mothers, health workers and clinicians are the next
step (Section~\ref{sec:future}).
